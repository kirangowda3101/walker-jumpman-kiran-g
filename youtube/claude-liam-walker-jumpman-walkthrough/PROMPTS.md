# PROMPTS — The Fork That Wasn't.

The two prompts that appear on screen, verbatim, and what is true about each.

---

## B00 — the cold-open ask (illustrative reconstruction)

> Please use Walker to convert my game design document about a compact 2D
> precision platformer — a hooded runner crossing two gaps and a spike field on
> a short greybox course, unlimited instant retries, and a branching final zone
> that makes the player choose a route to the flag — into a playable Godot
> project.

**Status: reconstruction, not history.** No Walker transcript exists for this
project and none is implied. The repository is an *extension* of
[nikbearbrown/walker-jumpman](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/walker-jumpman),
whose design package (`GDD.md`, `LEVEL-DESIGN.md`, `BUILD-REPORT.md` and the
rest) is retained unmodified in the repo. This prompt is written to describe
**the game that actually shipped**, so the opening frames the real artefact
rather than a fiction.

The reconstruction is declared three times so a viewer cannot miss it:

1. the first output line on screen — "Illustrative reconstruction of the ask.
   Not a saved transcript.";
2. Liam says it aloud in B00 — "That prompt is an illustrative reconstruction,
   not a saved transcript";
3. here, and in `SOURCES.md`.

No fictional build log, progress bar, file-write receipt or elapsed-time
readout appears anywhere in the film.

The on-screen output lines are **facts about the shipped slice**, not invented
tool output:

| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| "Shipped slice: one level, 1920 px wide, 12 solids, 5 spike clusters." | `godot/levels/first_steps.json` |
| "Starter: nikbearbrown/walker-jumpman · extended by Kiran Gowda Ramanagara Jayaram." | `SOURCES.md` |

## BHTF — the Your Turn prompt

> Use Walker on my Godot platformer: replace the route fixture's right-only
> driver with one that takes a timed axis sequence, then generate and verify a
> fixture that drives the ground route's backtrack. Prove it by failing when I
> weaken jump_velocity.

**Why this prompt.** It is the project's largest open gap, named by the author
rather than invented for the film. `godot/tests/route_driver.gd` can only hold
the right key:

```gdscript
func step(player: CharacterBody2D) -> void:
    player.test_control = true
    player.test_axis = 1.0
    ...
```

so it physically cannot drive the ground route's westward backtrack.
`TEST-REPORT.md` §7.3 records the consequence: *"The ground route is verified by
my own play and by the solver, not by a scripted input. A driver that accepts an
axis sequence rather than only right would fix this and is not implemented."*

The film shows the ground route completing — but under a capture driver written
for the film, not under the project's test suite. The prompt closes exactly that
gap.

**Why the last clause matters, and what Liam says about it.** The narration
reads the prompt in full and then spends two lines on the final sentence: a
fixture that can only ever pass tells you nothing, and one you can break on
demand is evidence. The project already demonstrates this pattern —
`scripts/check_reachability.py --jump-velocity 190` turns a PASS into a FAIL
with a specific reason — so the prompt asks for the same falsifiability on the
route fixture.

**The concrete experiment for the viewer:** run it on their own Godot project,
then weaken a movement constant and confirm the fixture actually fails. If it
still passes, the fixture was never testing what they thought.

## Prompts NOT used

No prompt/code/revision loop is forced onto this film. `godot-waikthrough` owns
the middle of the reel and the middle is real gameplay; the Claude interface
appears only at the cold open, the verdict artifact page, the handoff and the
outro card. There are no ask→result pairs around the gameplay beats, because the
gameplay was not generated by a prompt — it was played.
